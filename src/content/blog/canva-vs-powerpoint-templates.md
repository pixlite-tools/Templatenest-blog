Search "presentation template" on any marketplace and you'll get a wall of results split between two very different file types: Canva templates and PowerPoint (.pptx) templates. They can look nearly identical in the preview image and behave completely differently once you open them. Here's the actual difference, and how to pick.

## The core difference

**Canva templates** live inside Canva's browser-based editor. You need a Canva account (free tier works for most templates), and editing happens through drag-and-drop panels rather than a traditional slide-software interface.

**PowerPoint templates** are native `.pptx` files. They open in Microsoft PowerPoint, Keynote (with some conversion), or Google Slides (with some layout drift), and behave like any deck you've built from scratch.

Neither is "better" in the abstract — the right choice depends on three things: where you'll present it, who else needs to edit it, and how much control you want over the fine details.

## Choose Canva if…

- **You're not presenting live in PowerPoint or Keynote.** Canva decks work great for social carousels, PDFs, or screen-shares from a browser tab.
- **You want speed over precision.** Canva's drag-and-drop panels are faster for swapping colors, photos, and layouts, especially if design isn't your day job.
- **Your team already lives in Canva.** If your brand kit, colors, and fonts are already set up there, a Canva template slots in immediately.
- **You need built-in stock assets.** Canva's photo and icon library is baked into the editor — no hunting for images elsewhere.

## Choose PowerPoint if…

- **You're presenting in a room with a projector or on a client's machine.** `.pptx` is still the universal format for live presenting, and it doesn't require an internet connection or a Canva login to run.
- **You need exact control over animations and transitions.** PowerPoint's animation pane is more granular than anything Canva currently offers.
- **You're pitching investors, executives, or anyone likely to ask for the "editable file."** Sending a native `.pptx` avoids the "can you also send me the PowerPoint version" follow-up email.
- **Your organization has strict brand templates (master slides).** PowerPoint's slide master system handles enforced brand consistency across large decks better than Canva's page-by-page approach.

## The hybrid approach

A growing number of buyers use both: a Canva version for quick internal drafts and social use, and a PowerPoint version of the same deck for the final client-facing or investor presentation. If a template only comes in one format, check whether the seller offers file conversion, or plan to rebuild the slide master by hand if you switch platforms later.

## What to check before buying either

1. **Font licensing.** Canva Pro fonts and premium PowerPoint fonts sometimes aren't licensed for commercial redistribution once exported. Check the product description.
2. **Placeholder count vs. your actual content.** A 12-slide template with only 6 layout variations will mean a lot of manual rework if your deck runs to 20 slides.
3. **Editable vs. locked elements.** Some cheaper templates lock background graphics — fine for speed, frustrating if you need full control.
